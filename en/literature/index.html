Bibliography

Sources cited in each lecture and the scope of their use.

Lecture 1 · What a state is

K. Landsman, Foundations of Quantum Theory (2017), Def. 4.11 and Thm 4.12, pp. 109–110; the extension argument in Prop. C.15, p. 652, and Lemma C.4, p. 646; B. C. Hall, Quantum Theory for Mathematicians (2013), Defs. 19.6–19.7 and Thm 19.9, pp. 423–425. Hall uses sequential strong continuity in place of our projection-additivity axiom. The proof above constructs the representing operator by its ONB coefficients and uses Landsman’s Cauchy–Schwarz tail estimate.

Lecture 2 · One state, many preparations

Busch–Lahti–Pellonpää–Ylinen, Quantum Measurement (2016), Thm 9.1, pp. 193–194; Example 9.1, p. 195; Thm 9.2, p. 196 (a more general decomposition statement, with its own kernel hypothesis). Nielsen–Chuang, Quantum Computation and Quantum Information (2010), Thm 2.6, pp. 103–104 (finite unitary freedom and zero padding). Landsman, Foundations of Quantum Theory (2017), pp. 46–48 (finite-dimensional version). K. Mølmer, Phys. Rev. A 55, 3195–3203 (1997), especially pp. 3195–3197; reply, 58, 4247 (1998). The coherent-state expansion is QM-II, Theorem 8.10.

Lecture 3 · Probabilities on effects

Landsman, Foundations of Quantum Theory, Thm 2.44 pp. 73–74 (finite extension proof), §2.10 pp. 75–77, Thm 4.29 p. 120 (Gleason). P. Busch, Phys. Rev. Lett. 91, 120403 (2003), arXiv:quant-ph/9909073. R. Sorkin, Mod. Phys. Lett. A 9, 3119–3128 (1994), arXiv:gr-qc/9401003. U. Sinha et al., Science 329, 418–421 (2010), arXiv:1007.4193. The infinite-dimensional normality step uses L1 directly, not a trace-duality theorem.

Lecture 4 · Symmetries are unitary or antiunitary

Galindo–Pascual, Quantum Mechanics I, §7.2 pp. 262–266; Weinberg, The Quantum Theory of Fields I, App. 2.A pp. 91–96; Moretti, Spectral Theory and Quantum Mechanics, Thm 12.11 pp. 675–681. The real-linearity/global-sign completion of the anchor proof is supplied here. The ring operator and its eigenbasis are recalled from QM-II, Theorem 3.10; the line operators from Theorem 4.6.

Lecture 5 · Time, and the symmetries that forbid superpositions

Moretti, Spectral Theory and Quantum Mechanics, Theorem 12.45 pp. 714–715, Definition 12.40 p. 708; Pauli discussion pp. 827–828 (our proof is the direct lower-bound argument, with the displayed course sign convention). Superselection: §7.7 pp. 378–379, §12.3.4 p. 782; Weinberg, The Quantum Theory of Fields I, §2.7 pp. 81–90. Busch–Lahti–Pellonpää–Ylinen, Quantum Measurement, pp. 390, 395. Experimental and mass-rule citations accompany the optional applications in the full edition.

Lecture 6 · Composite systems in infinite dimension: EPR

Hall, Quantum Theory for Mathematicians, Theorem 19.13 pp. 430–431; Moretti, Spectral Theory and Quantum Mechanics, §13.4 pp. 844–858. The matrix compactness and normal-state proofs above supply the needed prerequisites explicitly. Einstein–Podolsky–Rosen, Phys. Rev. 47, 777 (1935); Duan et al., PRL 84, 2722 (2000). The optical experiment and its reference are in the full edition.

Lecture 7 · Channels: complete positivity and Stinespring

Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information, Theorem 2.22 pp. 82–84 (opposite Choi factor order); Heinosaari–Ziman, The Mathematical Language of Quantum Theory, pp. 186–189, 207–209 (also an output-first convention in Theorem 4.48); Nielsen–Chuang, §8.2 pp. 360–376; Gustafson–Sigal, Mathematical Concepts of Quantum Mechanics, 3rd ed. (2020), Theorems 19.21, 19.24 pp. 260–265. The explicit infinite-series and continuous-expectation arguments above use L1 and L6.

Lecture 8 · Open-system dynamics: the GKSL equation

Rivas–Huelga, Open Quantum Systems, pp. 5–7, Theorem 4.2.1 pp. 35–39; Weinberg, Lectures on Quantum Mechanics, §6.9 pp. 237–243; Nielsen–Chuang, p. 386 (their Lindblad normalisation differs by 2\sqrt22​); Schlosshauer, Decoherence and the Quantum-to-Classical Transition, pp. 155–167. The forward proof includes its own finite product estimate.

Lecture 9 · Readings beyond spectral measures: POVMs

Busch–Lahti–Pellonpää–Ylinen, Quantum Measurement, pp. 71, 146, 168–175, 269–270, 443–447; Busch–Lahti–Mittelstaedt, The Quantum Theory of Measurement, §III.2.6 pp. 41–43; Heinosaari–Ziman, Proposition 3.25 pp. 123–124. QM-II’s cited spectral theorem is the input to the explicitly sketched Q,PQ,PQ,P argument; coherent vectors are its Theorem 8.10.

Lecture 10 · Instruments: the state after a reading

Busch–Lahti–Pellonpää–Ylinen, Quantum Measurement, §7.7 pp. 159–161, Theorem 7.14 p. 160; conditioning pp. 211–212; position model pp. 241–243, equations (10.32)–(10.33) p. 242; repeatability Theorem 10.4 p. 248. Busch–Lahti–Mittelstaedt, pp. 41–43, 84–86. The direct density-operator conditioning proof and the fully specified qubit sequence are supplied here.

Lecture 11 · Decoherence: the unread environment

Schlosshauer, Decoherence and the Quantum-to-Classical Transition, pp. 88–93, 119–138; saturation equations (3.45)–(3.48) p. 129, Rayleigh coefficient (3.66)–(3.67) p. 134 and Table 3.1 p. 135. The air calculation uses the saturated collision rate, not an extrapolation of Table 3.2’s quadratic values. The explicit channel proof uses L1, L6 and L7.

Lecture 12 · The measurement problem

BLPY, Quantum Measurement, Theorems 22.1–22.2 pp. 491–494; Landsman, Foundations of Quantum Theory, Theorems 11.2, 11.4 pp. 447–449; Tumulka, Foundations of Quantum Mechanics, pp. 93–94, 172–176 (the latter critically analyses the FR argument); Maudlin, Topoi 14, 7–15 (1995). Von Neumann, Mathematical Foundations of Quantum Mechanics, pp. 272–273, 285, for the chain/cut discussion. Primary extended-friend papers are named in the text with their premises.

Lecture 13 · Values added: contextuality, locality, Bohm

Peres, Quantum Theory: Concepts and Methods, equation (7.1) p. 189, pp. 196–200; Bell, Speakable and Unspeakable, 2nd ed., ch. 1 pp. 2–4; Mermin, PRL 65, 3373 (1990); Cabello, Estebaranz and García-Alcaine, Phys. Lett. A 212, 183 (1996); Jarrett, Noûs 18, 569–589 (1984), pp. 578–583; Dürr–Teufel, Bohmian Mechanics, pp. 152–153, 158–161; Tumulka, pp. 28–29, 79–80, 212–213. Experimental and global-existence primary sources are identified in the text.

Lecture 14 · Collapse and branching: GRW and Everett

Tumulka, Foundations of Quantum Mechanics, pp. 104–109, 124–129, 232–234 (his width convention has rC=2σr_C=\sqrt2\sigmarC​=2​σ; our GRW/Bassi choice rC=10−7 mr_C=10^{-7}\,\mathrm mrC​=10−7m corresponds to σ≃7.1⋅10−8 m\sigma\simeq7.1\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm mσ≃7.1⋅10−8m); Ghirardi–Rimini–Weber, PRD 34, 470 (1986); Bassi et al., RMP 85, 471 (2013). Wallace, The Emergent Multiverse, pp. 94, 174–182, 308–311; Griffiths, Consistent Quantum Theory, pp. 137–147, 216–219. The explicit tail bound and finite capstone identity are supplied here.
