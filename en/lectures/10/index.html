Lecture 10

Instruments: the state after a reading

What this lecture does

A POVM predicts a reading’s probabilities but does not determine the state left for the next experiment. Lüders’ update follows from a precise conditioning premise on all sub-events. An instrument specifies both statistics and state changes. We construct it from a pointer, compute the position pointer’s added spread, and compare two full sequences with the same first POVM.

10.1 Lüders as conditioning on every sub-event

QM-I, Postulate 4.1, postulated the ideal state update for a sharp reading. We now ask which probability-conditioning premise characterises it. Let ρ\rhoρ be a density operator on nonzero separable H\HilH, and EEE a projection with p=Tr⁡(ρE)>0p=\Tr(\rho E)>0p=Tr(ρE)>0.

Theorem 10.1 (Lüders conditioning). There is exactly one density operator τ\tauτ satisfying Tr⁡(τE)=1,Tr⁡(τE′)=Tr⁡(ρE′)pfor every projection E′≤E.\Tr(\tau E)=1,\qquad \Tr(\tau E')=\frac{\Tr(\rho E')}{p} \quad\text{for every projection }E'\leq E.Tr(τE)=1,Tr(τE′)=pTr(ρE′)​for every projection E′≤E. It is τ=EρE/p\tau=E\rho E/pτ=EρE/p. The first condition is also the case E′=EE'=EE′=E of the second.

Proof. Expand ρ=∑jrj∣φj⟩ ⁣⟨φj∣\rho=\sum_jr_j\proj{\varphi_j}ρ=∑j​rj​∣φj​⟩⟨φj​∣ by L1. The positive series EρE=∑jrj∣Eφj⟩ ⁣⟨Eφj∣E\rho E=\sum_jr_j\proj{E\varphi_j}EρE=∑j​rj​∣Eφj​⟩⟨Eφj​∣ has trace ∑jrj∥Eφj∥2=Tr⁡(ρE)=p\sum_jr_j\norm{E\varphi_j}^2=\Tr(\rho E)=p∑j​rj​∥Eφj​∥2=Tr(ρE)=p, so its normalisation is a density operator. It has support in EEE. For E′≤EE'\leq EE′≤E, pairing its series with E′E'E′ gives the required conditional probability, since EE′=E′E=E′EE'=E'E=E'EE′=E′E=E′. Conversely expand a candidate τ=∑jtj∣χj⟩ ⁣⟨χj∣\tau=\sum_jt_j\proj{\chi_j}τ=∑j​tj​∣χj​⟩⟨χj​∣ in positive spectral terms. The identity Tr⁡(τ(11−E))=0\Tr(\tau(\Id-E))=0Tr(τ(11−E))=0 says that the sum of the nonnegative weights times ∥(11−E)χj∥2\norm{(\Id-E)\chi_j}^2∥(11−E)χj​∥2 vanishes. Every nonzero weighted χj\chi_jχj​ lies in ran⁡E\operatorname{ran}EranE, hence τ=EτE\tau=E\tau Eτ=EτE. For every unit u∈ran⁡Eu\in\operatorname{ran}Eu∈ranE, use E′=∣u⟩ ⁣⟨u∣E'=\proj uE′=∣u⟩⟨u∣ to obtain ⟨u,τu⟩=⟨u,ρu⟩/p\inner u{\tau u}=\inner u{\rho u}/p⟨u,τu⟩=⟨u,ρu⟩/p. Homogeneity extends equality of these quadratic forms to every vector in that subspace. Complex polarisation determines all their matrix elements there, and both candidate operators vanish on the perpendicular complement. Thus τ=EρE/p\tau=E\rho E/pτ=EρE/p. 

The premise concerns every subprojection, not just the elements of one chosen basis. Merely asking that the outcome EEE become certain permits many other states in its range when rank⁡E≥2\operatorname{rank}E\geq2rankE≥2. The theorem also does not assert that every real apparatus implements this conditioning rule, or explain a dynamical physical collapse. It characterises a rule once the premise is imposed. At p=0p=0p=0 it supplies no normalised conditional state. The proof works for a qubit and infinite-rank EEE; it uses no Gleason dimension restriction (compare BLPY pp. 211–212).

10.2 An instrument carries probabilities and outputs

Definition 10.2 (Instrument). For countable outcomes an instrument is a family of CP maps Ij\mathcal I_jIj​ on T1(H)\mathcal T_1(\Hil)T1​(H) whose sum, convergent in every bounded expectation pairing, is a channel. For real Borel outcomes it is a family B↦I(B)B\mapsto\mathcal I(B)B↦I(B) of CP maps, with total map I(R)\mathcal I(\R)I(R) a channel and countable additivity in every bounded pairing Tr⁡(AI(B)(ρ))\Tr(A\mathcal I(B)(\rho))Tr(AI(B)(ρ)). For a density input, the outcome probability is p(B)=Tr⁡I(B)(ρ)p(B)=\Tr\mathcal I(B)(\rho)p(B)=TrI(B)(ρ); if it is positive, the conditional state is I(B)(ρ)/p(B)\mathcal I(B)(\rho)/p(B)I(B)(ρ)/p(B).

For explicit position computations we use the intervals and finite unions of L9. A full Borel position-pointer model uses the cited Borel spectral framework recalled in QM-II, §5.1; no measure-extension proof is claimed here. The general model argument below starts with a PVM already given. The sum or total map describes the unread experiment. Each outcome operation is generally trace decreasing on positive inputs, not a channel by itself. Normalising it makes the conditional rule nonlinear; its unnormalised CP map is what composes simply with the next operation.

The quadratic-form Cauchy–Schwarz argument gives ∣b(φ,ψ)∣≤∥φ∥∥ψ∥|b(\varphi,\psi)|\leq\norm\varphi\norm\psi∣b(φ,ψ)∣≤∥φ∥∥ψ∥. As in L1, set uN=∑n≤Nb(en,ψ)enu_N=\sum_{n\leq N}b(e_n,\psi)e_nuN​=∑n≤N​b(en​,ψ)en​ in one ONB. BLPY, Theorem 10.1 p. 227, implements a POVM; the instrument theorem also fixes the output operations. Its general proof is not our finite Choi theorem. In finite dimension, adding an outcome register to L7’s Kraus isometry gives the familiar construction. This establishes possible models, not uniqueness of an apparatus behind its statistics.

The physics

Repeated ideal tests illustrate how an instrument matters to dynamics: QM-II’s discrete Zeno computation uses its short-time survival law for ψ∈D(H)\psi\in D(H)ψ∈D(H) and increasingly frequent selected projections. It is not an assertion that every physical continuous detector freezes the system. Finite Ozawa computations and the repeated-test example remain optional Sheet 10 material under the course’s scope ruling.

What is the state after a reading?

An observation is an instrument. Its POVM supplies probabilities, and its normalised positive-probability operations supply conditional states. Lüders’ state is uniquely selected by conditioning every sharp sub-event. Every instrument has a pointer model, by the cited representation theorem. Two instruments with one POVM can give different later probabilities and states; resolution and unread disturbance also belong to the apparatus model rather than to a mean operator alone.

Summary

Sub-event conditioning proves Lüders’ uniqueness without a dimension exception. Pointer projection sandwiches induce CP additive instruments. The Gaussian position instrument has a continuous Kraus density; ignoring its record adds a reflected momentum-kick convolution. The centred unchirped pointer obeys ϵQηP=ℏ/2\epsilon_Q\eta_P=\hbar/2ϵQ​ηP​=ℏ/2. The capstone computes how a changed first instrument alters the rest of an otherwise fixed sequence.
