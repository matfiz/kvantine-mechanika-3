Lecture 2

One state, many preparations

A density operator fixes expectations, not a preparation history. The extreme points of the set of density operators are exactly the rays. When dim⁡H≥2\dim\Hil\geq2dimH≥2, even a ray leaves some bounded observable uncertain. A unitary on padded preparation labels relates any two finite pure-state ensembles of the same state. For one optical mode, averaging the phase of a coherent state gives exactly a Poisson mixture of number states.

2.1 What mixing forgets

Theorem 1.6 identified a state with the unique density operator that gives its bounded expectations. Uniqueness of the operator does not mean uniqueness of the preparation. In QM I, §3.6, half the spins prepared up and half down along zzz gave the same state as a preparation using the two xxx directions. The same distinction survives in infinite dimension. We keep the nonzero separable complex Hilbert space H\HilH of Lecture 1 and write D(H)\mathcal D(\Hil)D(H) for its density operators.

Definition 2.1 (Convexity and pure states). A set is convex if it contains tρ1+(1−t)ρ2t\rho_1+(1-t)\rho_2tρ1​+(1−t)ρ2​ whenever it contains ρ1,ρ2\rho_1,\rho_2ρ1​,ρ2​ and 0≤t≤10\leq t\leq10≤t≤1. A point ρ\rhoρ in a convex set is extreme if, for ρ1,ρ2\rho_1,\rho_2ρ1​,ρ2​ belonging to that same set, ρ=tρ1+(1−t)ρ2,0<t<1⟹ρ1=ρ2=ρ.\rho=t\rho_1+(1-t)\rho_2,\quad 0<t<1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \rho_1=\rho_2=\rho.ρ=tρ1​+(1−t)ρ2​,0<t<1⟹ρ1​=ρ2​=ρ. A pure state is an extreme point of D(H)\mathcal D(\Hil)D(H); a state that is not pure is mixed.

The density operators form a convex set: a convex combination is bounded and positive, and its diagonal sum in any ONB is tTr⁡ρ1+(1−t)Tr⁡ρ2=1t\Tr\rho_1+(1-t)\Tr\rho_2=1tTrρ1​+(1−t)Trρ2​=1. Its mean of a bounded AAA is tTr⁡(ρ1A)+(1−t)Tr⁡(ρ2A)t\Tr(\rho_1A)+(1-t)\Tr(\rho_2A)tTr(ρ1​A)+(1−t)Tr(ρ2​A), by the basis expression for the trace proved in §1.2. Physically, select one preparation with probability ttt and the other with probability 1−t1-t1−t, and discard the selection record. Retaining that record is additional information about the preparation, not part of the density operator of this system alone.

The restriction 0<t<10<t<10<t<1 matters. A pure state can occur in a mixture of two identical copies of itself; extremality only excludes a nontrivial mixture of different states. Nor does an extreme point have to be isolated: the pure qubit states form the boundary sphere of the qubit’s Bloch ball. The question is whether a line segment joining different states can have this point strictly between its endpoints.

2.3 Pure does not mean sharp for everything

For bounded self-adjoint AAA, its variance in state ρ\rhoρ is Var⁡ρ(A)=Tr⁡(ρA2)−[Tr⁡(ρA)]2=Tr⁡(ρ(A−Tr⁡(ρA)11)2)≥0.\operatorname{Var}_\rho(A)=\Tr(\rho A^2)-[\Tr(\rho A)]^2 =\Tr\bigl(\rho(A-\Tr(\rho A)\Id)^2\bigr)\geq0.Varρ​(A)=Tr(ρA2)−[Tr(ρA)]2=Tr(ρ(A−Tr(ρA)11)2)≥0. It is the mean square deviation of its readings. Here AAA is bounded, so both expectations fall within L1’s theorem and exist without any extra moment assumption. A state is dispersion-free for AAA when this variance vanishes, and dispersion-free when it vanishes for every bounded self-adjoint AAA.

Proposition 2.3 (No universally sharp quantum state). If dim⁡H≥2\dim\Hil\geq2dimH≥2, every density operator gives some rank-one projection EEE a strictly positive variance. No state is dispersion-free on all bounded observables.

Proof. Since E2=EE^2=EE2=E, put w=Tr⁡(ρE)w=\Tr(\rho E)w=Tr(ρE); then Var⁡ρ(E)=w(1−w)\operatorname{Var}_\rho(E)=w(1-w)Varρ​(E)=w(1−w). If ρ\rhoρ is mixed, Theorem 2.2 gives at least two positive eigenvalues, each less than one. Choose one, p∈(0,1)p\in(0,1)p∈(0,1), and a unit eigenvector eee. For E=∣e⟩ ⁣⟨e∣E=\proj eE=∣e⟩⟨e∣, w=pw=pw=p lies strictly between 0 and 1.

If ρ=∣ψ⟩ ⁣⟨ψ∣\rho=\proj\psiρ=∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣ is pure, choose a unit φ⊥ψ\varphi\perp\psiφ⊥ψ, possible because dim⁡H≥2\dim\Hil\geq2dimH≥2. Let χ=(ψ+φ)/2\chi=(\psi+\varphi)/\sqrt2χ=(ψ+φ)/2​ and E=∣χ⟩ ⁣⟨χ∣E=\proj\chiE=∣χ⟩⟨χ∣. Its probability is w=∣⟨χ,ψ⟩∣2=1/2w=|\inner\chi\psi|^2=1/2w=∣⟨χ,ψ⟩∣2=1/2, so its variance is 1/41/41/4. 

The order of quantifiers is important: for every state there is an uncertain event. It does not say that one fixed projection is uncertain in every state. A unit vector in its range gives it probability one; a unit vector in its kernel gives probability zero. On a one-dimensional space every bounded observable is a scalar and the unique state is dispersion-free, which explains the dimension hypothesis.

For example, a spin prepared in ∣0⟩\ket0∣0⟩ has zero variance for σz\sigma_zσz​ and variance one for σx\sigma_xσx​. Being pure excludes uncertainty arising from a nontrivial convex preparation of that state; it does not exclude uncertainty in a particular reading. This result concerns quantum states and their expectations. Whether an enlarged theory can attach further values to a system is a different question, taken up in Lecture 13. No claim about all such theories has been proved here.

2.4 The unitary freedom of finite ensembles

Definition 2.4 (An ensemble of a state). An ensemble of ρ\rhoρ is a finite or countable list {(pi,ψi)}\{(p_i,\psi_i)\}{(pi​,ψi​)} of probabilities and unit vectors such that ∑ipi=1\sum_i p_i=1∑i​pi​=1 and ρ=∑ipi∣ψi⟩ ⁣⟨ψi∣\rho=\sum_i p_i\proj{\psi_i}ρ=∑i​pi​∣ψi​⟩⟨ψi​∣. The vectors need not be orthogonal or distinct. Zero-probability entries may be omitted.

For a countable list this series converges in operator norm, since the norm of its tail is at most ∑i>Npi\sum_{i>N}p_i∑i>N​pi​. Its limit is positive with trace one: summing its diagonal entries interchanges only nonnegative terms, as in L1. Every state has at least its spectral ensemble. A finite ensemble, however, has finite-rank density operator even when the ambient Hilbert space is infinite-dimensional. The next theorem concerns finite ensembles only.

Absorb each weight into its vector, writing xi=piψix_i=\sqrt{p_i}\psi_ixi​=pi​​ψi​. Then ρ=∑i∣xi⟩ ⁣⟨xi∣\rho=\sum_i\ketbra{x_i}{x_i}ρ=∑i​∣xi​⟩⟨xi​∣, with ∑i∥xi∥2=1\sum_i\norm{x_i}^2=1∑i​∥xi​∥2=1. These vectors are not generally unit vectors. A zero-weight entry is the zero vector; recovering a unit vector from it is unnecessary.

The unitary acts on the list of preparation labels. It is not a unitary operation on the physical system sending the unit vectors of one ensemble to those of the other. Padding is substantive. The one-entry list (ψ)(\psi)(ψ) and the two-entry list (ψ/2,ψ/2)(\psi/\sqrt2,\psi/\sqrt2)(ψ/2​,ψ/2​) generate the same pure state. The physics

A photon-counting description of a laser mode often uses Poisson weights, while a calculation of interference uses a coherent amplitude and a phase. Equation (2.3) explains one precise equivalence between these descriptions. It does not derive the state produced by a particular laser. Mølmer’s 1997 paper calls the absence of optical coherences in optics experiments a conjecture. His 1998 reply discusses ordinary discharge lasers and the entanglement of the pump electrons with the lasing atoms; it also explicitly allows optical coherence in principle. Those are physical arguments about sources. The mathematical equality here neither settles them nor forbids phase-sensitive measurements with a reference.

Uniform averaging is an additional assumption about how phase is represented when the reference is unavailable. A fixed-phase coherent state and ρav\rho_{\mathrm{av}}ρav​ are generally different states. For example, when α>0\alpha>0α>0 is real, the bounded operator A=∣h0⟩ ⁣⟨h1∣+∣h1⟩ ⁣⟨h0∣A=\ketbra{h_0}{h_1}+\ketbra{h_1}{h_0}A=∣h0​⟩⟨h1​∣+∣h1​⟩⟨h0​∣ has mean 2e−α2α2e^{-\alpha^2}\alpha2e−α2α in ψα\psi_\alphaψα​, but zero in ρav\rho_{\mathrm{av}}ρav​. Thus the lesson is not that phase can never matter. It is that the phase-averaged state has two ensemble descriptions. A reference or a retained preparation record changes what information is available and must be included in a full model.

What does a state say about its preparation?

A normal state fixes all bounded expectations. It is pure exactly when it is a ray, and mixed states can have many ensembles, even with distinct eigenvalues. Finite ensembles of the same state are related by a unitary on their padded label lists. No state on a space of dimension at least two is sharp for every bounded observable. The phase-averaged coherent model and a Poisson number mixture are one state; this does not select a preparation history or determine how an actual laser works.

Summary

Pure means extreme; in dimension at least two it does not mean dispersion-free. A spectral decomposition is one preparation description, not a uniquely privileged one. HJW makes the finite-ensemble freedom exact; the laser identity identifies a uniform phase family and a discrete number-state ensemble with one state by a separate calculation. Later, the same care about what a density operator does not specify will be essential when we discuss reduced states and decoherence. Next we ask whether probabilities assigned directly to events or effects already force the trace rule.
