Lecture 11

Decoherence: the unread environment

What this lecture does

An environment can record alternatives without anyone reading its record. The resulting channel suppresses interference in a part while the combined state can remain pure. We compute this for a qubit and for position-dependent random kicks, then distinguish the small-separation and resolved-collision regimes before estimating two physical decoherence times.

11.3 Which scattering regime does the Gaussian model describe?

The Gaussian channel is mathematically valid for every separation. Its identification with a scattering experiment has a restricted regime. The following kinetic derivation is sketched, following Schlosshauer, §3.1–3.4 pp. 119–138. It assumes a massive object with negligible recoil, independent stationary scattering events, and negligible free motion during the coherence comparison. A symmetric incident bath removes directed phase drift. These are physical assumptions, not consequences of CP.

Let Γ\GammaΓ be the total collision rate and qqq the momentum-transfer component along the separation. In one event the coherence gains a factor χ(d)=⟨eiqd/ℏ⟩\chi(d)=\langle e^{iqd/\hbar}\rangleχ(d)=⟨eiqd/ℏ⟩ from the transfer distribution. During a short interval, the probabilities of no collision and one collision are 1−Γ dt1-\Gamma\,dt1−Γdt and Γ dt\Gamma\,dtΓdt to first order. Thus ρ˙(x,x′)=Γ(χ(x−x′)−1)ρ(x,x′).\dot\rho(x,x')=\Gamma(\chi(x-x')-1)\rho(x,x').ρ˙​(x,x′)=Γ(χ(x−x′)−1)ρ(x,x′). For a symmetric distribution with finite second moment and ∣qd/ℏ∣≪1|qd/\hbar|\ll1∣qd/ℏ∣≪1 at typical transfers, χ(d)≃1−⟨q2⟩d22ℏ2,Λdec=Γ⟨q2⟩2ℏ2.\chi(d)\simeq1-\frac{\langle q^2\rangle d^2}{2\hbar^2},\qquad \Lambda_{\mathrm{dec}}=\frac{\Gamma\langle q^2\rangle}{2\hbar^2}.χ(d)≃1−2ℏ2⟨q2⟩d2​,Λdec​=2ℏ2Γ⟨q2⟩​. This is the quadratic small-separation law. If one uses the total three-dimensional transfer magnitude in an isotropic bath, its component second moment is one third of that total; the factor must be converted.

At separations resolved by the scattered particles, the overlap factor in the cited isotropic model averages close to zero. The decay rate then approaches Γ\GammaΓ, rather than increasing without limit as d2d^2d2. The wavelength language is an order-of-magnitude criterion; the precise small parameter is transfer times separation divided by ℏ\hbarℏ. Saturation is an asymptotic property of the specified scattering model, not a universal monotonicity theorem for every conceivable kick distribution. One must not extrapolate the quadratic coefficient to a macroscopically resolved separation just because its Gaussian channel remains positive.

In the controlled-qubit model, the interaction preserves the system basis 0,10,10,1 and makes its superpositions entangle with distinct records. A pointer basis here consists of vectors ∣j⟩\ket j∣j⟩ for which U(∣j⟩⊗e)=∣j⟩⊗ejU(\ket j\otimes e)=\ket j\otimes e_jU(∣j⟩⊗e)=∣j⟩⊗ej​: they remain products while their superpositions can entangle with records. Position-dependent scattering similarly favours localised packets or coarse position sectors. Exact delta functions are not pointer states in this Hilbert space. The widths, stability and competition with free spreading depend on the interaction and timescale; decoherence does not select one universal basis for every Hamiltonian.

The physics

Hornberger et al., PRL 90, 160401 (2003), observed loss of fullerene interference with increasing background-gas pressure (doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.90.160401). This supports collisional decoherence, with an apparatus-specific scattering model. It is separate from our model dielectric sphere’s thermal-photon estimate. Environmental coherence loss changes an interference contrast; seeing that contrast disappear alone does not establish selection of one global outcome.

Why are position superpositions difficult to observe?

A part coupled to an initially separate, unread environment evolves by a channel. Records suppress its interference while the joint state can remain pure. In the stated scattering regime the small-separation decay rate is Λdecd2\Lambda_{\mathrm{dec}}d^2Λdec​d2; resolved collisions instead give a rate near the total collision rate. Position probabilities stay fixed in this pure-dephasing approximation. The reduced mixture supplies no unique selected outcome; that remaining question is the measurement problem.

Summary

Environmental overlap determines the qubit’s coherence. L7’s scalar construction gives a genuine Gaussian kick channel, whose kernel factor is proved in the appropriate L2L^2L2 sense. Quadratic diffusion and resolved-collision saturation describe different physical regimes. The two estimates illustrate why assumptions and scales precede the numerical rate. Proper and improper refer to preparation context; neither local density statistics nor diagonalisation by itself chooses an outcome.
