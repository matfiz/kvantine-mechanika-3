Lecture 5

Time, and the symmetries that forbid superpositions

Time translations supply more structure than separate symmetries. Their phases can be coordinated, by a cited theorem, into the unitary group of QM-II. A lower energy bound then excludes an operator that translates energy by arbitrary amounts. Rotations raise a different question: which rays belong to one coherent sector, and which observables can compare them?

5.1 From ray translations to a Hamiltonian

Lecture 4 classified each symmetry separately. A time evolution must also compose consistently: evolving for sss, then ttt, is evolving for s+ts+ts+t. We assume a nonzero separable complex H\HilH, with dimension at least two. The one-dimensional case has only one ray and no nontrivial ray dynamics.

There are three distinct assumptions in this chain. Transition probabilities determine the kind of each symmetry by Wigner. Neither closed-system unitarity nor a time-independent Hamiltonian describes every reduced system: tracing out a partner will lead to different evolution in L7. 5.3 Rotations and coherent sectors

Time translations have a group lift. Rotations illustrate a different possibility. QM-I, Theorem 7.3, gives for each finite spin multiplet a rotation by 2π2\pi2π as (−1)2j11(-1)^{2j}\Id(−1)2j11: plus for integer jjj, minus for half-integer jjj. Each is a common phase within its own multiplet, hence acts trivially on its rays, as the identity rotation in ordinary three-dimensional space must do.

Call a subspace coherent for a projective rotation action if all its rays are admitted as pure states and the ordinary rotation group SO(3)SO(3)SO(3) acts on them with a well-defined projective action. Here SO(3)SO(3)SO(3) means the ordinary proper rotations of space; “projective” again allows one common phase for each implementing operator, not a separate phase for each component of a vector. We assume that rotations about zzz are implemented, up to that common phase, by the spin operators e−iθJz/ℏe^{-i\theta J_z/\hbar}e−iθJz​/ℏ of those multiplets. This connects the ray action to the stated physical angular momentum; an unrelated abstract rotation action would not suffice. Integer and half-integer blocks may be orthogonal sums of finite multiplets: their unitary rotations act blockwise and extend by norm limits of finite component sums. Thus the 2π2\pi2π signs remain +1+1+1 and −1-1−1 on the respective whole blocks.

Suppose one such subspace contained nonzero integer and half-integer components u,vu,vu,v. They are orthogonal spin sectors. Their sum transforms under 2π2\pi2π as u+v↦u−vu+v\mapsto u-vu+v↦u−v. These are different rays: if u−v=a(u+v)u-v=a(u+v)u−v=a(u+v), comparison of the orthogonal components gives both a=1a=1a=1 and a=−1a=-1a=−1. But 2π2\pi2π is the identity element of SO(3)SO(3)SO(3) and must fix every ray. This proves the univalence restriction: one coherent sector carrying the stated projective action cannot contain both types.

The vector u+vu+vu+v certainly exists in the direct-sum Hilbert space. The conclusion depends on which rays and which group action are physically admitted. Using the group of spin rotation operators instead changes the premise: in that group the 2π2\pi2π spin rotation is not the identity. A group name without its action on states is insufficient to establish a superselection rule. To describe the restricted observations explicitly, we now specify an algebra rather than silently discarding the Hilbert-space vectors.

Both branches here have half-integer spin; this experiment does not superpose integer and half-integer sectors. The controlled one-arm operation is not the identity spatial rotation acting globally on the system.

The physics

Rauch et al., Phys. Lett. A 54, 425–427 (1975), and Werner et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 35, 1053 (1975), observed this spinor sign using crystal neutron interferometers and magnetic precession in one beam. The ideal calculation assumes equal path amplitudes, a fixed relative phase at zero rotation, and no path-dependent loss or spin decoherence. Unpolarised spin gives the same cosine after averaging the two σz\sigma_zσz​ eigenstates. Experimental visibility and background require apparatus information beyond this model.

The physics

A conditional mass rule uses QM-II’s Weyl relation. At a fixed time, take translation U(a)=e−iaP/ℏU(a)=e^{-iaP/\hbar}U(a)=e−iaP/ℏ and boost V(mv)=eimvQ/ℏV(mv)=e^{imvQ/\hbar}V(mv)=eimvQ/ℏ. Their loop is U(−a)V(−mv)U(a)V(mv)=e−imav/ℏ11.U(-a)V(-mv)U(a)V(mv)=e^{-imav/\hbar}\Id.U(−a)V(−mv)U(a)V(mv)=e−imav/ℏ11. The corresponding ordinary Galilei transformations commute in this loop. If one projective action of that ordinary group covers every admitted ray, the identity loop must be one common phase. Two different masses acquire different phases for generic avavav, so their coherent sum fails this premise, just as the two spin types did. This is Bargmann’s conditional mass rule, not a prohibition obtained from a mass label alone. Enlarging the group by an extra phase parameter changes the premise; see Giulini, Ann. Phys. 249, 222–235 (1996), arXiv:quant-ph/9508002.

Why unitary time evolution, and which superpositions?

A continuous real group of ray symmetries has, by the cited lift, a strongly continuous unitary group. With Stone’s correspondence its action is ρ↦UtρUt†\rho\mapsto U_t\rho U_t^\daggerρ↦Ut​ρUt†​, Ut=e−itH/ℏU_t=e^{-itH/\hbar}Ut​=e−itH/ℏ, with HHH self-adjoint and fixed up to an energy constant. A lower bound excludes a self-adjoint time that translates energy globally. A superselection rule specifies a smaller observable algebra; its states are expectation rules on that algebra, and its pure states need not be all Hilbert-space rays.

Summary

The group premise, probability continuity and cited lift explain the structure of time evolution. Pauli’s proof needs global covariance with domains, not a formal commutator. The projective rotation premise separates integer and half-integer coherent sectors. Their block algebra has unique block-diagonal density representatives, and its pure states are sector rays. A neutron’s relative rotation measures a 4π4\pi4π path fringe without comparing the two superselected spin types.
