Lecture 9

Readings beyond spectral measures: POVMs

What this lecture does

A pointer can have noisy statistics that are not a spectral measure of the system alone. Effects describe those probabilities. We prove that every countable family of such effects can be obtained by a sharp reading on a larger system, derive the effects of a position pointer, and identify the obstruction to a joint sharp position–momentum reading.

9.1 Probabilities need effects, not necessarily projections

QM-I’s probe (Example 11.11) already formed Fj=Kj†KjF_j=K_j^\dagger K_jFj​=Kj†​Kj​ and called its family a POVM. These are the effects defined generally in Lecture 3. No requirement made Fj2=FjF_j^2=F_jFj2​=Fj​. For a whole reading procedure, its events must fit together into one normalised probability assignment for every state.

For elementary real-outcome calculations, events are intervals and their finite disjoint unions. In phase space use rectangles and finite disjoint unions of rectangles. An additivity statement assumes its union belongs to this event class. The general cited theorems below use Borel sets, the larger class generated by countable unions and complements; their full event-space framework is a cited input, not a measure-extension construction supplied here.

Definition 9.1 (Positive operator-valued measure). For countably many outcomes, a POVM is a family of effects Fj≥0F_j\geq0Fj​≥0 with ∑jFj=11\sum_jF_j=\Id∑j​Fj​=11 strongly. Its outcome probabilities are pj=Tr⁡(ρFj)p_j=\Tr(\rho F_j)pj​=Tr(ρFj​). For real or phase-space outcomes it assigns effects F(B)F(B)F(B) to event sets, with F(∅)=0F(\varnothing)=0F(∅)=0, F(whole space)=11F(\text{whole space})=\IdF(whole space)=11, and, for every disjoint countable event family, ⟨ψ,F(⋃jBj)ψ⟩=∑j⟨ψ,F(Bj)ψ⟩.\inner\psi{F(\bigcup_j B_j)\psi} =\sum_j\inner\psi{F(B_j)\psi}.⟨ψ,F(j⋃​Bj​)ψ⟩=j∑​⟨ψ,F(Bj​)ψ⟩. A POVM is sharp when its effects form a projection-valued measure (PVM), with F(B∩C)=F(B)F(C)F(B\cap C)=F(B)F(C)F(B∩C)=F(B)F(C); otherwise it is unsharp.

This is QM-II’s interval spectral-measure structure with multiplicativity dropped. Finite additivity extends to finite unions, and countable additivity is required whenever the union is still an admissible event.

Positivity gives 0≤F(B)≤110\leq F(B)\leq\Id0≤F(B)≤11 by its complement. The countable axiom is deliberately weaker than multiplicativity. L1’s spectral density expansion and interchange of nonnegative sums show it gives countably additive scalar probabilities for every density operator, not just rays. A joint phase-space POVM has marginals F1(I)=F(I×R)F_1(I)=F(I\times\R)F1​(I)=F(I×R) and F2(J)=F(R×J)F_2(J)=F(\R\times J)F2​(J)=F(R×J).

The mean operator alone loses information. For 0<η<10<\eta<10<η<1, a noisy qubit reading with numerical outputs ±1\pm1±1 has effects F±=(11±ησz)/2F_\pm=(\Id\pm\eta\sigma_z)/2F±​=(11±ησz​)/2 and first moment F+−F−=ησzF_+-F_-=\eta\sigma_zF+​−F−​=ησz​. The sharp operator A=ησzA=\eta\sigma_zA=ησz​ has that same mean operator, but its outputs are ±η\pm\eta±η with spectral projections (11±σz)/2(\Id\pm\sigma_z)/2(11±σz​)/2. In ∣0⟩\ket0∣0⟩ the noisy reading gives ±1\pm1±1 with probabilities (1±η)/2(1\pm\eta)/2(1±η)/2, while the sharp one gives +η+\eta+η with certainty. Their second moment operators are 11\Id11 and η211\eta^2\Idη211 respectively. Thus specifying a first moment does not specify the reading’s statistics. The identification of an observable with one self-adjoint operator includes the additional assertion that its statistics are sharp.

9.4 Joint sharp readings must commute

For Q,PQ,PQ,P the relevant noncommutation is spectral, not merely a formal commutator on a test domain. Here is the spectral argument, sketched using QM-II’s cited spectral calculus. If every position projection commuted with every momentum projection, it would commute with U(a)=e−iaP/ℏU(a)=e^{-iaP/\hbar}U(a)=e−iaP/ℏ. But this unitary translates a wave packet from an interval III to I+aI+aI+a. Choose a nonzero smooth packet supported in III and a translation with disjoint intervals: projecting before or after translation gives different results. Thus some spectral projections do not commute. There is no joint POVM with both exact QQQ and exact PPP as marginals. Joint unsharp readings remain possible.

The physics

Ideal eight-port homodyne detection, with its parameter input in the vacuum and the two readout quadratures set 90∘90^\circ90∘ apart, implements this phase-space reading in the strong-local-oscillator limit (BLPY, §19.4, Proposition 19.2 pp. 443–445). The parameter-port vacuum supplies the additional reading blur; another input there changes the POVM. Finite local-oscillator amplitude and inefficient detectors require further qualifications or smearing. The Gaussian above is the prediction for the ideal coherent input and ideal POVM, not a universal detector response. The extra vacuum contribution belongs to the reading as well as to the prepared state; counting the vacuum only in the state, or only in the reading, gives half of each observed variance.

What does a pointer read?

A reading’s statistics are a POVM. Every countable POVM is a projective reading of the system with an ancilla; real-outcome dilation is cited. Sharp statistics are the spectral measures of self-adjoint operators. Joint sharp marginals must commute, so exact QQQ and PPP cannot both be marginals of one reading. A calibrated pointer and the specified eight-port detection instead supply explicit unsharp effects. A POVM specifies probabilities; the state after a recorded outcome needs an instrument.

Summary

Effects extend sharp spectral readings. Square roots yield countable Naimark dilation, with an enlarged space ensuring unitary completion. The shear pointer gives a reflected convolution kernel. Projection domination proves the sharp joint-reading constraint. The coherent-state POVM gives a Gaussian phase-space output, with separate preparation and reading contributions to its variance.
