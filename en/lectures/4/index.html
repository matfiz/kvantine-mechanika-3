Lecture 4

Symmetries are unitary or antiunitary

What this lecture does

A bijection of all rays preserving transition probabilities comes from a unitary or an antiunitary operator. Its type is global, and its only remaining freedom is a phase. Complex conjugation supplies time reversal on the line; on a flux-threaded ring it reverses both momentum and flux. Every member of a one-parameter group of ray symmetries has unitary type.

4.1 What must a symmetry preserve?

QM-I called a unitary commuting with the Hamiltonian a symmetry (Definition 6.3). It also introduced an antiunitary time reversal (Definition 7.6). The two notions were not the same: time reversal is antilinear, so it was not a symmetry in the sense of Definition 6.3. Here we ask the kinematic question before specifying a Hamiltonian. Which changes of pure states preserve the probabilities for comparing them?

Definition 4.1 (Ray symmetry). The ray [ψ][\psi][ψ] of a nonzero vector is its one-dimensional complex span. For two rays their transition probability is p([ψ],[φ])=∣⟨ψ,φ⟩∣2∥ψ∥2∥φ∥2.p([\psi],[\varphi])= \frac{|\inner\psi\varphi|^2}{\norm\psi^2\norm\varphi^2}.p([ψ],[φ])=∥ψ∥2∥φ∥2∣⟨ψ,φ⟩∣2​. A ray symmetry (or Wigner symmetry) is a bijection S\mathcal SS of all rays preserving these transition probabilities.

Rescaling either vector cancels between numerator and denominator. For unit vectors the probability is the expectation of ∣φ⟩ ⁣⟨φ∣\proj\varphi∣φ⟩⟨φ∣ in ∣ψ⟩ ⁣⟨ψ∣\proj\psi∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣, as in QM-I and L2. Orthogonal rays have transition probability zero; identical rays have probability one. Both relations are therefore preserved by a symmetry and its inverse. The definition is about all rays of the specified space. Restrictions to coherent sectors will be stated separately in Lecture 5.

Definition 4.2 (Antiunitary). An antiunitary V:H→H\mathcal V:\Hil\to\HilV:H→H is an onto antilinear map such that V(aψ+bφ)=aˉVψ+bˉVφ,⟨Vψ,Vφ⟩=⟨ψ,φ⟩‾.\mathcal V(a\psi+b\varphi)=\bar a\mathcal V\psi+\bar b\mathcal V\varphi, \qquad \inner{\mathcal V\psi}{\mathcal V\varphi} =\overline{\inner\psi\varphi}.V(aψ+bφ)=aˉVψ+bˉVφ,⟨Vψ,Vφ⟩=⟨ψ,φ⟩​. A unitary is onto, linear and preserves the inner product itself.

Both kinds preserve norms and transition probabilities, and hence induce Wigner symmetries. Multiplying an implementation by a complex number of modulus one changes no image ray. It remains to prove the converse and to rule out a map that makes an independent linear/antilinear choice for each vector. That last consistency question is the main work.

4.2 Wigner’s theorem: fixing an anchor ray

On a separable complex Hilbert space with dim⁡H≥2\dim\Hil\geq2dimH≥2, every Wigner symmetry is induced by a unitary or antiunitary V\mathcal VV, meaning S[ψ]=[Vψ]\mathcal S[\psi]=[\mathcal V\psi]S[ψ]=[Vψ] for every nonzero ψ\psiψ. 6. Prove uniqueness, including the type. If two implementations have the same type, their quotient is linear and fixes every ray. The theorem is kinematic: it does not say that every such map commutes with the energy or is an available time evolution.

The physics

Persistent currents were measured through the magnetic response of mesoscopic metallic rings: for example, Lévy et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 64, 2074 (1990), and Chandrasekhar et al., 67, 3578 (1991). Our calculation is one spinless particle on a clean ring, not a quantitative model of a disordered many-electron sample. It isolates the symmetry prediction: reversing flux reverses the current on corresponding nondegenerate branches. Populations, disorder and interactions must be specified to predict an experimental amplitude.

4.5 A group in time cannot contain an antiunitary

On dim⁡H≥2\dim\Hil\geq2dimH≥2, suppose ray symmetries satisfy Ss+t=SsSt\mathcal S_{s+t}=\mathcal S_s\mathcal S_tSs+t​=Ss​St​ for all real s,ts,ts,t. Each is implemented by Wigner’s theorem, but St=(St/2)2.\mathcal S_t=(\mathcal S_{t/2})^2.St​=(St/2​)2. The square of a unitary is linear; the square of an antiunitary is also linear, because conjugation of scalar coefficients occurs twice. Both squares are unitary. Type uniqueness in Theorem 4.3 therefore forces every St\mathcal S_tSt​ to have unitary type. Continuity has not even been used in this argument; it will be needed for the generator in L5.

On a qubit, conjugation in the σz\sigma_zσz​ basis sends ρ\rhoρ to ρˉ=ρT\bar\rho=\rho^{\mathsf T}ρˉ​=ρT and its Bloch vector to (rx,−ry,rz)(r_x,-r_y,r_z)(rx​,−ry​,rz​). It preserves transition probabilities, but it has antiunitary type. It cannot occur as one member of such a real one-parameter group. This does not make time reversal unphysical: time reversal changes the orientation of time, whereas the group of time translations moves forward or backward within that orientation.

Integer time labels would be different: set the ray map to the identity at every even integer and to conjugation at every odd integer. Since conjugation squares to the identity, these maps form a group over Z\ZZ and include an antiunitary type. Extending it to all real times would require a half-step whose square had that type, which Wigner forbids. The conclusion is about a group with arbitrarily divisible time labels, not a prohibition of every discrete antilinear transformation.

The remaining obstruction is phase. Choosing a unitary implementation separately at every time need not give Us+t=UsUtU_{s+t}=U_sU_tUs+t​=Us​Ut​; it gives that identity only up to a phase. Wigner alone has not derived the evolution postulate. Lecture 5 states the additional continuity premise and the lifting theorem that remove this obstruction.

For a concrete example of uncoordinated phases, take any genuine group UtU_tUt​ and a fixed time scale τ>0\tau>0τ>0. The representatives Vt=ei(t/τ)2Ut\mathcal V_t=e^{i(t/\tau)^2}U_tVt​=ei(t/τ)2Ut​ induce exactly the same ray maps, but VsVt=e−2ist/τ2Vs+t.\mathcal V_s\mathcal V_t =e^{-2ist/\tau^2}\mathcal V_{s+t}.Vs​Vt​=e−2ist/τ2Vs+t​. This follows by subtracting (s+t)2(s+t)^2(s+t)2 from s2+t2s^2+t^2s2+t2. Each representative is unitary and depends continuously on time, yet the chosen family is not a group. Multiplying back by e−i(t/τ)2e^{-i(t/\tau)^2}e−i(t/τ)2 restores the group. Coordination of phases is therefore an extra step; continuity of an arbitrary choice of representatives does not by itself supply the group law.

Which changes of pure states are symmetries?

For dimension at least two, a bijection of all rays preserving transition probabilities has one unitary or antiunitary implementation, unique up to phase and with a unique type. A real one-parameter group contains only unitary-type maps, but its individual phases still have to be coordinated. Time reversal is antiunitary and reverses momenta and magnetic flux; its domain action is part of each operator statement.

Show answers

The half-time map must exist; an integer-indexed group could have antiunitary-type maps at odd steps.

Summary

Wigner’s theorem derives the allowed linear and antilinear forms from ray probabilities. The anchor proof establishes one global type, even in infinite dimension. On a ring, time reversal pairs opposite twists, levels and currents; the current derivative needs a nondegenerate branch. The square argument excludes antiunitaries from a real translation group. The group law up to phase remains to be addressed.
