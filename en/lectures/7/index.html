Lecture 7

Channels: complete positivity and Stinespring

What this lecture does

A part of a closed system need not evolve unitarily. Its state change must remain positive when the part is entangled with another system. In finite dimension, with trace preservation, that requirement is equivalent to a positive Choi matrix, a Kraus sum and a unitary evolution with an initially separate environment. We also construct the infinite-dimensional channels needed later.

7.1 Positivity must survive a spectator

A preparation with an ignored classical label gives a convex mixture. We assume a complex-linear evolution map on the span of density operators. In finite dimension that span is all matrices. In a separable H\HilH, use L1’s T1(H)\mathcal T_1(\Hil)T1​(H), the complex span of positive trace-class operators. Complete positivity is a condition on that map, not on a single output state.

7.3 A channel is a unitary with an ignored environment

In infinite dimension the orthogonal-complement argument needs a further hypothesis or a larger environment: two isomorphic infinite-dimensional subspaces need not have isomorphic complements. The theorem just proved is finite-dimensional. We next prove sufficient infinite constructions; the general representation theorem will remain cited.

What can happen to an open system?

A channel is a complex-linear, completely positive, trace-preserving state map. In finite dimension it is exactly a part of a unitary evolution with an independently prepared environment. Countable Kraus sums and the specified continuous unitary mixtures also define channels on separable spaces. An initially correlated preparation needs its own joint-state specification; a system marginal alone need not determine its future.

Summary

The spectator test excludes transpose as a local channel. A positive finite Choi matrix yields Kraus operators by explicit reshaping; their completeness sum makes the dilation an isometry and permits a finite unitary extension. Infinite sufficient constructions need convergence and normality, not a formal replacement of finite sums by integrals. Spontaneous emission reduces population and coherence by different factors while its system and environment together may remain pure.
