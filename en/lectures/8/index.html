Lecture 8

Open-system dynamics: the GKSL equation

What this lecture does

A channel describes one change of state. Modelling changes by a continuous, time-homogeneous semigroup imposes more structure. In finite dimension its generator has the GKSL form. We prove that this form generates channels, sketch why it is necessary, and derive the relation between population relaxation and coherence decay for a qubit.

A continuous semigroup on a finite-dimensional vector space has a bounded linear generator L\mathcal LL with Φt=etL\Phi_t=e^{t\mathcal L}Φt​=etL and ρ˙=L(ρ)\dot\rho=\mathcal L(\rho)ρ˙​=L(ρ) (cited: Rivas–Huelga, Theorem 1.3.1 pp. 5–6 and Corollary 1.3.1 p. 7). Our forward proof starts with a specified L\mathcal LL and proves its channel property directly, without using this cited converse-to-continuity step as an input.

That experiment is not a direct measurement of the qubit relation (8.5). Finally, a semigroup is an assumption about the physical regime. Born, Markov and often secular or coarse-graining approximations enter microscopic reservoir derivations; none is proved by writing a GKSL expression. QM-II’s short-time law (Proposition 12.8) requires a unit vector in D(H)D(H)D(H); its cited Khalfin theorem (Theorem 12.9) also requires a lower energy bound. In QM-II’s Friedrichs decay model the excited population is exactly that closed-system survival probability: the initial excited vector is the only excited-system sector. It starts quadratically, rather than with a nonzero exponential slope, and cannot remain exponentially bounded at arbitrarily late times. Thus e−t/T1e^{-t/T_1}e−t/T1​ is an effective intermediate-time model there, with 1/T1≈Γ1/T_1\approx\Gamma1/T1​≈Γ from the cited golden rule (QM-II, Theorem 12.13). A generic reduced population is not automatically a survival probability; its relation to the closed dynamics must be checked, not used to evade the decay model’s short- and long-time restrictions. For infinite systems later lectures build their channels explicitly; any differential generator statements have their own domain qualifications.

How does an open system evolve in time?

A finite-dimensional open system modelled by a continuous time-homogeneous semigroup of channels obeys ρ˙=L(ρ)\dot\rho=\mathcal L(\rho)ρ˙​=L(ρ) with a GKSL generator. The forward implication is proved here and the converse sketched. The semigroup is a physical modelling assumption. Closed evolution is the case with no Lindblad terms. Infinite-dimensional generators require additional hypotheses beyond this finite theorem.

Summary

CP short steps converge in norm to the finite GKSL exponential; trace preservation is checked separately. Conditional Choi positivity explains the converse structure. Population and coherence decays give 1/T2=1/(2T1)+γϕ1/T_2=1/(2T_1)+\gamma_\phi1/T2​=1/(2T1​)+γϕ​. The unital qubit shows why CP is stronger than positivity. Exact subsystem semigroup behaviour and exact closed-system survival are different claims.
