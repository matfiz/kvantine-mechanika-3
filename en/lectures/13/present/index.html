Lecture 13

Values added: contextuality, locality, Bohm

Giving up completeness C means adding physical facts to the state. Those facts cannot simply be noncontextual values of every observable. The Peres–Mermin square proves the obstruction. Bell locality separates into two model-relative conditions. Bohmian mechanics illustrates the cost: additional positions with contextual readings and, in equilibrium, the quantum position distribution preserved by the guiding flow.

13.1 Which rules should individual values obey?

Expectation values add: ω(A+B)=ω(A)+ω(B)\omega(A+B)=\omega(A)+\omega(B)ω(A+B)=ω(A)+ω(B), even when A,BA,BA,B do not commute. That does not mean the outcome of reading A+BA+BA+B is a sum of outcomes in one joint reading of AAA and BBB. For Pauli matrices σx,σz\sigma_x,\sigma_zσx​,σz​, each spectrum is {+1,−1}\{+1,-1\}{+1,−1}, but (σx+σz)2=211(\sigma_x+\sigma_z)^2=2\Id(σx​+σz​)2=211, so their sum’s spectrum is {+2,−2}\{+\sqrt2,-\sqrt2\}{+2​,−2​}. Individual spectral values of the two terms add to −2,0,2-2,0,2−2,0,2, none of these. A value rule additive on this noncommuting pair is therefore impossible.

Von Neumann’s no-hidden-values argument assumes an additivity requirement beyond commuting families; Bell’s critique targets that physical assumption (von Neumann pp. 201–202; Bell, ch. 1 p. 4). It does not invalidate the mathematics under its stated assumptions. L3’s effect additivity likewise concerns probabilities, not a license to add deterministic eigenvalues of incompatible observables.

A noncontextual value assignment here chooses a spectral value v(A)v(A)v(A) for the listed observables, independent of the commuting family in which it is read, and respects functional relations within each such family. In particular it respects its products. The usual condition v(f(A))=f(v(A))v(f(A))=f(v(A))v(f(A))=f(v(A)) implies those rules for finite commuting matrices: assume the value rule also applies to their self-adjoint functions, including the auxiliary matrix below. Simultaneous diagonalisation gives common eigenspace projections (Er)(E_r)(Er​) and a matrix D=∑rdrErD=\sum_rd_rE_rD=∑r​dr​Er​ with distinct drd_rdr​. Every member is a function of DDD, and v(AB)=(fg)(v(D))=f(v(D))g(v(D))=v(A)v(B)v(AB)=(fg)(v(D))=f(v(D))g(v(D))=v(A)v(B)v(AB)=(fg)(v(D))=f(v(D))g(v(D))=v(A)v(B). No rule for a product of noncommuting operators has been introduced.

Theorem 13.1 (Kochen–Specker contradiction on two qubits). There is no assignment of context-independent values v(Ajk)∈{±1}v(A_{jk})\in\{\pm1\}v(Ajk​)∈{±1} respecting the six commuting-triple products of (13.1).

Proof. Multiplying the three required row-value products gives +1+1+1. Multiplying the three required column-value products gives −1-1−1. Each expression is the product of the same nine scalar values, once each. They cannot differ. No noncommuting operator product was used in this scalar contradiction. 

This proof is in dimension four. Dimension three also has Kochen–Specker constructions, including Peres’s 33 real rays; Cabello’s 18 rays give another dimension-four construction (named only). In finite dimension at least three, the impossibility of assigning zero or one to every projection, adding to one on each orthogonal resolution, also follows from the cited Gleason theorem of L3 and L2’s absence of a dispersion-free quantum state; that route depends on the cited theorem. The square is the independent finite proof supplied here. A single qubit escapes this theorem: L3 constructed a two-valued projection assignment, and Bell gives a qubit hidden-value model (ch. 1 pp. 2–3). A statement about all quantum dimensions must retain this distinction.

The same square supplies a statistic. On different runs read each of the six contexts. Let RjR_jRj​ be its row outcome product and CkC_kCk​ its column outcome product, and define the sum of their separately sampled context means ⟨χ⟩=⟨R1⟩+⟨R2⟩+⟨R3⟩+⟨C1⟩+⟨C2⟩−⟨C3⟩.\begin{equation} \label{eq:13:stat} \langle\chi\rangle=\langle R_1\rangle+\langle R_2\rangle+\langle R_3\rangle +\langle C_1\rangle+\langle C_2\rangle-\langle C_3\rangle. \end{equation}⟨χ⟩=⟨R1​⟩+⟨R2​⟩+⟨R3​⟩+⟨C1​⟩+⟨C2​⟩−⟨C3​⟩.(13.2) For one noncontextual nine-value assignment write χ\chiχ for the corresponding scalar sum. Then the product of these six signed terms is −1-1−1: each value occurs twice and the last term adds one minus sign. At least one term is −1-1−1, so χ≤4\chi\leq4χ≤4. A probability mixture, with its assignment distribution independent of the context chosen, retains ⟨χ⟩≤4\langle\chi\rangle\leq4⟨χ⟩≤4. Quantum theory instead makes each signed context-product operator 11\Id11, giving ⟨χ⟩=6\langle\chi\rangle=6⟨χ⟩=6 for every density operator. The six contexts are separate experimental choices, not a single joint reading of nine noncommuting observables.

The physics

Kirchmair et al., Nature 460, 494 (2009), arXiv:0904.1655, used two trapped calcium ions and sequential compatible readings. Their reported singlet statistic was 5.46±0.045.46\pm0.045.46±0.04, above four. The test requires stable implementations of each observable across its contexts, and control of compatibility and disturbance. The ideal bound excludes the specified deterministic noncontextual assignments and their mixtures; it is not an assumption-free bound on every noisy stochastic response model. The paper analyses its experimental disturbance conditions.

13.3 Locality is a property of a model

Take settings x,yx,yx,y, outcomes α,β=±1\alpha,\beta=\pm1α,β=±1 and hidden state λ\lambdaλ. Allow either countable Λ\LambdaΛ with preparation weights, or a subset of Rd\R^dRd with an ordinary probability density, extending QM-I, L12’s finite case and density remark. The distribution of λ\lambdaλ is assumed independent of the later settings. This setting independence is a separate premise. A model specifies normalised nonnegative responses pλ(α,β∣x,y)p_\lambda(\alpha,\beta|x,y)pλ​(α,β∣x,y), averaged over that fixed distribution to give the observed probabilities. Conditions may fail on a set of hidden states of probability zero, which changes no averaged prediction; use one common such exception set for the finite CHSH choices. Define marginals by summing the other outcome.

Definition 13.2 (Parameter and outcome independence). Parameter independence (PI) says the marginals are independent of the remote setting; denote their common values by pλ(α∣x,y)=pλ(α∣x)p_\lambda(\alpha|x,y)=p_\lambda(\alpha|x)pλ​(α∣x,y)=pλ​(α∣x) and pλ(β∣x,y)=pλ(β∣y)p_\lambda(\beta|x,y)=p_\lambda(\beta|y)pλ​(β∣x,y)=pλ​(β∣y). Outcome independence (OI) means pλ(α,β∣x,y)=pλ(α∣x,y)pλ(β∣x,y).p_\lambda(\alpha,\beta|x,y)=p_\lambda(\alpha|x,y)p_\lambda(\beta|x,y).pλ​(α,β∣x,y)=pλ​(α∣x,y)pλ​(β∣x,y). Bell locality means there exist normalised local response distributions rλ(1)(α∣x),rλ(2)(β∣y)r^{(1)}_\lambda(\alpha|x),r^{(2)}_\lambda(\beta|y)rλ(1)​(α∣x),rλ(2)​(β∣y) such that pλ(α,β∣x,y)=rλ(1)(α∣x)rλ(2)(β∣y)p_\lambda(\alpha,\beta|x,y)=r^{(1)}_\lambda(\alpha|x)r^{(2)}_\lambda(\beta|y)pλ​(α,β∣x,y)=rλ(1)​(α∣x)rλ(2)​(β∣y).

Proposition 13.3 (Bell locality ⟺\Longleftrightarrow⟺ PI and OI). For the specified response probabilities, factorisation holds exactly when PI and OI both hold.

Proof. Sum the factorisation over either outcome to identify its normalised local factors with the corresponding marginals and obtain PI; substituting those marginals gives OI. Conversely substitute the setting-independent marginals of PI into the OI product to obtain normalised local factors. 

The OI definition has no division by zero. When the conditioning marginal is positive, it equivalently says pλ(α∣β,x,y)=pλ(α∣x,y)p_\lambda(\alpha|\beta,x,y)=p_\lambda(\alpha|x,y)pλ​(α∣β,x,y)=pλ​(α∣x,y), and similarly on the other side. A zero marginal already forces its associated joint probabilities to be zero by positivity. Setting independence was not proved by this equivalence and is still needed to infer the usual Bell bound. Jarrett’s term “completeness” for OI is not L12’s completeness C; we use PI/OI to keep that distinction visible.

For the ordinary quantum singlet model λ=ψ−\lambda=\psi^-λ=ψ−, p(α,β∣x,y)=14(1−αβ ax⋅by),p(α∣x,y)=p(β∣x,y)=12.p(\alpha,\beta|x,y)=\tfrac14(1-\alpha\beta\,\mathbf a_x\cdot\mathbf b_y),\qquad p(\alpha|x,y)=p(\beta|x,y)=\tfrac12.p(α,β∣x,y)=41​(1−αβax​⋅by​),p(α∣x,y)=p(β∣x,y)=21​. Here ax,by\mathbf a_x,\mathbf b_yax​,by​ are the two measurement axes of QM-I. Thus PI holds. At equal axes p(+,+)=0p(+,+)=0p(+,+)=0, whereas the product of the marginals is 1/41/41/4; OI fails. This is compatible with QM-I’s proved no-signalling statement: the unconditional local marginal is unchanged by the remote choice. OI concerns conditioning on the remote outcome.

A deterministic model may have outputs A(x,y,λ),B(x,y,λ)A(x,y,\lambda),B(x,y,\lambda)A(x,y,λ),B(x,y,λ) depending on both settings. Its joint point distribution equals the product of its two point marginals, so OI holds automatically. If it also obeyed PI and setting independence, it would satisfy the CHSH bound proved in QM-I. Its pointwise bound averages unchanged over a convergent countable preparation sum, since the summands are bounded and the nonnegative weights sum to one. Hence a deterministic model reproducing the singlet violation must violate PI under those premises. Its averaged marginals can nevertheless be nonsignalling: PI conditions on a finer model state than the experimental average. “No Bell-local model” is not a theorem that one can send a controllable faster-than-light signal.

13.5 What the theorems cost this theory

The additional position is an actual physical fact, so the wavefunction alone is not complete C in L12’s sense. Quantum equilibrium specifies the initial configuration density ∣ψ∣2|\psi|^2∣ψ∣2, and equivariance preserves it under the proved conditions. Extending agreement to general recorded observations requires modelling the apparatus configuration and its coupling; the scalar one-dimensional proof alone is not that general measurement theorem.

In Bohmian spin models, spin belongs to the spinor wavefunction and the apparatus interaction, not to an extra little vector attached to each particle. The velocity uses (ℏ/m)Im⁡(ψ†∇ψ)/(ψ†ψ)(\hbar/m)\operatorname{Im}(\psi^\dagger\nabla\psi)/(\psi^\dagger\psi)(ℏ/m)Im(ψ†∇ψ)/(ψ†ψ) in the corresponding model without a vector potential (cited: Dürr–Teufel, §8.4 pp. 158–161). A Stern–Gerlach outcome is a position record produced by that interaction. Different contexts can associate different outcomes to the same operator while preserving its quantum statistics. For two qubits, contextuality is required by the square’s premises. For a single spin one half it is a feature of the Bohmian apparatus model, not forced by a nonexistent qubit KS contradiction. For the single-spin mechanism, Tumulka’s inverted Stern–Gerlach example (§2.3.9 pp. 79–80, cited) reverses the magnet and exchanges the output labels to preserve the spin calibration. The two arrangements can then assign opposite calibrated outcomes to the same initial wavefunction and configuration. This account uses its spinor apparatus model and approximately separated packets, with spatial/velocity assumptions and small tails neglected; it is not derived from the scalar flow theorem above.

For Bell’s experiment the deterministic fine state includes wavefunction and configurations of both systems and the relevant apparatus. Settings remain independent experimental inputs in the Bell comparison. Averaging unknown apparatus positions without including them in λ\lambdaλ need not leave a deterministic response model; whether it does depends on the actual response functions. At the fully specified level OI holds, and reproducing the singlet violation forces PI to fail, while equilibrium averaged statistics remain nonsignalling. This is the precise model-relative locality cost, rather than a claim of controllable signalling or a premise shared by every proposed theory.

Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph’s theorem is named only: under preparation independence for arbitrarily many independent copies and reproduction of the relevant quantum predictions, an ontological model (a hidden-state preparation/response model of the kind defined above) assigns non-proportional pure states non-overlapping hidden-state preparation distributions (ψ\psiψ-onticity): in the countable case their overlap ∑λmin⁡{μψ(λ),μφ(λ)}\sum_\lambda\min\{\mu_\psi(\lambda),\mu_\varphi(\lambda)\}∑λ​min{μψ​(λ),μφ​(λ)} is zero, and in the density case the corresponding integral is zero (Tumulka, Theorem 5.6 pp. 212–213). It is not an assumption-free verdict about what a wavefunction must mean. The next lecture turns to changing U and changing D, on the same explicit-premise basis.

What does adding values cost?

Noncontextual values respecting commuting quantum products fail on the Peres–Mermin square. Bell locality is PI plus OI, with independent settings also needed in the Bell argument. Bohm adds configurations, uses contextual readings and reproduces equilibrium position distributions by equivariance; its deterministic Bell model violates PI while retaining nonsignalling averages. These statements specify the assumptions changed, not a verdict choosing one response to the measurement problem.

Summary

The square gives a finite state-independent contradiction and a statistic six against four. PI/OI separate the two parts of a factorised response without dividing by zero. The guiding current supplies a conditional one-dimensional equivariance proof with a genuine global distribution conclusion. Nodes and general global existence remain cited. Context and the full apparatus state matter when connecting those positions to readings.
