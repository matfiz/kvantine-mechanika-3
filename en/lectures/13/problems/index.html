First hint

Differentiate the logarithm of the packet with respect to position. Only its imaginary part contributes to the guiding velocity. Separate the real and imaginary parts of the complex width before solving the trajectory equation.

Further hint

The velocity is linear in position, so integrate the logarithmic change of a nonzero trajectory’s absolute value and then handle the zero trajectory separately. For density transport, compare the Gaussian width and the Jacobian of the trajectory map. This example is nowhere zero; list which hypotheses that makes easy rather than generalising to packets with nodes.
